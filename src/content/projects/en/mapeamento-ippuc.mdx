## Context and problem

The Curitiba Urban Planning and Research Institute (IPPUC) maintains high-resolution aerial orthophotos of the city. Inventorying road markings (crosswalks, arrows, stop lines) and other urban elements from these images is slow, manual work. Task 4 of AP[x] investigates an automatic detection pipeline that produces georeferenced layers usable by urban planning.

## Architecture and technical decisions

```ascii title="Pipeline"
 orthophoto (GeoTIFF)
        |
        v
 tile cropping  --------------------->  OBB annotation (X-AnyLabeling)
 (rasterio)                                     |
        |                                       v
        |                            YOLO11-OBB / YOLO26-OBB training
        v                                       |
 per-tile inference  <--------------------------+
        |
        v
 pixel boxes --> map coordinates (raster transform)
        |
        v
 vector layer (geopandas) --> IPPUC GIS
```

**Oriented boxes (OBB) instead of axis-aligned.** Crosswalks and arrows appear at any angle, following the direction of the roads. An axis-aligned box around a diagonal crosswalk encloses a lot of asphalt and overlaps its neighbors; the oriented box describes the object with four vertices and an angle.

**Comparison across YOLO generations.** YOLO11-OBB and YOLO26-OBB were evaluated on the same annotated set, to choose by measured result and not by the newest version. YOLO26-OBB was adopted as the project model.

**Georeferencing at the end of the pipeline.** The network works in pixel coordinates of each tile; the conversion to map coordinates uses the raster's own affine transform (`rasterio`), and the resulting geometries are assembled with `geopandas`.

## Implementation

My responsibility in Task 4 is the evolution of the model: reviewing studies on oriented detection in aerial imagery, structuring the training and testing cycles, and evaluating techniques that improve YOLO26-OBB performance on the orthophotos.
