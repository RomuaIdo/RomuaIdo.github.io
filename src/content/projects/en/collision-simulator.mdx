## Context and problem

Visualize momentum conservation and elastic and inelastic collisions: several balls with random masses colliding with each other and with the borders, with controls to turn on gravity or a center of mass that attracts the balls.

## Architecture and technical decisions

The code is split by responsibility: `physics.c` (integration, collisions, center of mass), `objects.c`, `graphics.c`, `input.c`, and a `state.h` with the simulation state.

The collision between two balls is resolved by an impulse along the contact normal $\hat{n}$. With normal relative velocity $v_{rel} = (\vec{v}_b - \vec{v}_a)\cdot\hat{n}$ and coefficient of restitution $e$ (entered by the user at startup):

$$
J = \frac{-(1+e)\,v_{rel}}{\dfrac{1}{m_a} + \dfrac{1}{m_b}}, \qquad
\vec{v}_a \leftarrow \vec{v}_a - \frac{J}{m_a}\hat{n}, \qquad
\vec{v}_b \leftarrow \vec{v}_b + \frac{J}{m_b}\hat{n}
$$

If $v_{rel} > 0$ the balls are already moving apart and no impulse is applied, which keeps them from getting stuck to each other.

## Implementation

The center of mass is recomputed every frame as a weighted average of positions and velocities. The collision sound volume is proportional to the magnitude of the relative velocity, capped at the SDL_mixer maximum.
