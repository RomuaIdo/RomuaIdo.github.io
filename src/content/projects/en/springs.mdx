## Context and problem

Show that the seemingly chaotic motion of coupled masses is a sum of normal modes, each oscillating at its own frequency.

## Architecture and technical decisions

For $N$ masses connected to each other and to the walls by springs, Newton's second law gives the linear system

$$
M\ddot{\vec{x}} + K\vec{x} = 0
$$

with $M$ diagonal (masses) and $K$ tridiagonal (spring constants). Assuming $\vec{x} = \vec{a}\cos(\omega t + \varphi)$, one arrives at the generalized eigenvalue problem

$$
K\vec{a} = \omega^2 M\vec{a}
$$

solved with `scipy.linalg.eigh(K, M)`. The eigenvalues give the frequencies $\omega_k = \sqrt{\lambda_k}$, and the initial position and velocity conditions define the amplitude and phase of each mode.

## Implementation

The code is split into initialization, update (assembling $K$ and $M$, normal modes, amplitudes, and phases), rendering, and plots. The user sets the number of masses, mass values, spring constants, and initial conditions; the screen shows the masses, the springs, and real-time plots of position, velocity, and acceleration.

## Results and next steps

Since the solution is analytic (a sum of modes), it accumulates no integration error over time. A next step would be to add damping, which makes the problem non-conservative.
